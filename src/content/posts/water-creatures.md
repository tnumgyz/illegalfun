It was one of those rare Seattle days when the sun feels almost indulgent, as though it knows it doesn't belong 
and lingers anyway, and we let him have that day — or maybe, in truth, we let it have us.

## The museum

The museum became our prelude, the slow inhale before something deeper. We wandered without urgency, 
our bodies close enough to feel but not quite touching, and stopped in front of a Brazilian artist's work 
that blurred human skin into landscape, curves melting into forests, textures dissolving into something 
intimate and primal. I could feel it stirring in me, that quiet recognition of the body as something wild, 
not just seen but experienced, and he stood beside me in silence, present in a way that felt almost physical. 

We lingered in front of destruction too — a tornado preserved in fragments, chaos arranged into something 
beautiful — and let a tiny woman with swaying teddy-bear earrings pull us through the meaning of it all, 
her voice soft but grounded, like a gentle thread. I remember thinking how strange it was to feel both thoughtful 
and undone at the same time.

But the unraveling didn't begin until we returned.

## The unraveling

The hotel room held us differently. It wasn't just a space — it was an invitation. Concrete and wood grounded the room, 
but the deep burgundy tones softened everything, like warmth waiting beneath restraint, and the wide windows poured in that 
silvery Northwest light until it wrapped us in something almost dreamlike. 

I trusted him, and he trusted me, and it was never spoken because it never needed to be. We let go of structure, 
of expectation, of who we were supposed to be, and instead we played — not casually, but fully, the way adults 
forget they're allowed to, the way bodies and minds open only when they finally feel safe enough.

The wine helped, just enough to soften the sharp edges of thought, and then came sensation amplified by 
sense-enhancers: colors deepened, touch lingered longer than it should have, and even silence took on weight. 
We found each other slowly at first, and then all at once.

There was something electric in the way we moved through the room, between the bed and the glass shower, our reflections 
catching in shifting light while water slid over skin and refracted into soft teal, gold, and faint threads of lavender. 
It wasn't just cleansing — it was awakening. Every droplet felt deliberate, every movement amplified, and for a moment 
we weren't even ourselves. We became something fluid, something instinctive — true water creatures. 
Then he transformed into the wolf, and I let myself become the forest spirit, watching, moving with him rather than around him. 
There was a kind of surrender in it, but not weakness — something older, deeper, as though our bodies remembered each other 
from somewhere neither of us could explain. Maybe that's why it felt so easy. So inevitable.

Our conversations unfolded in fragments between touches, between breath, words slipping out of places we didn't know 
existed — things neither of us had ever said aloud, truths raw and unpolished. We didn't hold back. We couldn't. 
We laughed carelessly, uncontrollably, and then, suddenly and without warning, there were tears — not sadness exactly, 
but release, because when the body opens, everything rises with it, even the quiet pain we pretend isn't there. 
And somehow it felt safe. Safe enough to be seen there. Pain softens when it's held with kindness.

## The gummy bears

Later, hunger arrived, not gently but playfully. I spotted the crystal jar first — gummy bears glowing in impossible colors, 
translucent and almost too perfect. He suggested something more fulfilling, chocolate, maybe nuts, but I couldn't let it go. 
They looked alive. So I waited until his attention drifted, and then I opened it. We still laugh about it, because it wasn't 
just a jar — it felt like I'd unlocked a Pandora's box. 

The light from the window poured through them, making each one shimmer like it held its own secret, and I picked one up, 
pineapple, bright and sparkling, and held it for a moment as if I had something important to say. I did. But the words never 
came. So many truths felt simple and yet refused to make sense in this world, so instead I gave in and devoured it slowly, 
hungrily, letting the flavor bloom across my senses like something newly discovered.

He watched me. Then he laughed. "You ate that gummy bear for no good reason." And we laughed so hard it felt like my whole 
body folded into it — tears, breathlessness, that uncontrollable kind of joy that makes everything feel raw and alive. 
But it wasn't for no reason. It tasted like happiness. Like light. And ever since, every gummy bear carries a trace of that 
moment, of being fully present, utterly undone.

We drifted back toward the window afterward, wrapped in warmth and sunlight, while outside people moved through their lives 
unaware and the trees swayed in a perfect rhythm — but inside, time slowed for us again. I studied him, not casually but 
intimately, fascinated by the way light settled in his eyes, gold shifting into amber and then deepening into something 
greener, softer, colors blending and dissolving and re-forming. I traced it slowly, almost obsessively, as if memorizing him. 
I wanted to keep that version of him, to carry it somewhere hidden for the days when things feel heavier.

## Back to the world

Before sunset, we forced ourselves back into the world. Walking felt like an adventure, slightly disoriented, distracted by 
the softness of air against skin, the way everything seemed just slightly altered. I didn't know where we were going. 
He did — or at least he pretended convincingly enough. We arrived just in time, disheveled, glowing, completely unbothered, 
and dinner felt rich in a different way: seafood tender and indulgent, buttery bread melting instantly, red wine thick 
and velvety against the tongue. I felt it all more intensely. Even the quiet.

Across from us, a couple sat in silence, their connection reduced to polite gestures, forced smiles saved only for photographs, 
and it unsettled me — that quiet distance, so common it almost feels inevitable. I didn't want that. Not this. Not what we had. 
Then he noticed another table, a group alive with laughter, wine spilling into the night, that effortless joy that doesn't 
need a photograph to exist, and I followed his gaze and smiled, because that — that was us.

When we returned to the room, the spell hadn't quite broken, but time had caught up. Packing felt like an intrusion, like 
something unfinished. That's when he remembered the small things we'd brought but never used — the black leather cuffs, soft, 
smooth, almost teasing in their simplicity, tied with a delicate bow that made them feel more playful than dangerous. 
My pulse shifted slightly as he stepped closer and slipped one around my wrist. It fell off immediately. We stared at it 
for half a second, then collapsed into laughter again — uncontrolled, unfiltered, real.

And just like that, we left, forgetting them behind. Or maybe leaving them there on purpose. Because something told me this wasn't an ending.

It was only the beginning.
